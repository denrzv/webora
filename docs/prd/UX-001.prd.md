# UX-001: Design direction sketches and selection
Status: PRD_READY

## Context / Problem
`docs/design/AUDIT.md` states the finding plainly: the browser gives *websites* a six-role colour
system with a WCAG contrast guard, and gives itself a bare `MaterialTheme {}`. `SiteSkinColorScheme`
is better specified than any browser-owned surface. `M6` exists to correct that, and it cannot start
until someone decides what the browser should look like.

Three candidate directions were rendered ahead of this ticket at `docs/design/directions/index.html`
— *Soft instrument*, *Get out of the way*, *The instrument frame* — each as 360 dp mockups of the
four surfaces in light and dark, annotated with palette, geometry, icon budget and, decisively, its
mechanism for keeping the registrable domain and TLS state browser-owned under `ADR-006`. That
candidate set is this ticket's **input**, not its conclusion.

The deliverable is therefore a decision, not code. Without one, `UX-002` cannot choose a token set,
`UX-003` cannot rebuild the chrome, and each would relitigate the appearance question while trying
to ship something else.

The decision carries a security half that ordinary design choices do not. `C1` in the audit is
explicit: a Chrome-style omnibox showing the domain inside the editable text field collapses browser
identity into a field whose value is also page-derived and user-editable. A direction may unify them
visually **only if** the displayed identity still comes from `securityPresentation` and a separate
browser-authored semantics node survives. That is what makes this a decision worth recording as an
ADR rather than a preference worth noting in a ticket.

## Goals
1. Record the selected direction and its amendments as a decision future work can rely on.
2. Make the selected direction's `C1` mechanism concrete enough that `UX-003` can implement it
   without reopening the question — including what would constitute violating it.
3. Record the rule that survives whichever direction won: Webora's palette, typography and shape
   scale are compiled into the app, with no path from a manifest value into them.
4. Leave a reader able to reconstruct why the other two directions lost, so the decision can be
   revisited on evidence rather than re-argued from taste.

## Non-goals
- Any code. No `WeboraTheme`, no tokens, no icons, no composable changes — those are `UX-002` and
  `UX-003`. This ticket's output is a document.
- Re-rendering or editing the candidate sketches. They are the input and stay as they are.
- Deciding the SiteSkin integrated chrome's appearance. `UX-005` is descoped; the integrated surface
  already has a theme projection and a contrast guard.
- Choosing the icon set or its source. `C6` says there is none on the classpath; that is `UX-002`.

## User stories
- As the implementer of `UX-002`, I open one ADR and know which palette, geometry and chrome heights
  to build tokens for.
- As the implementer of `UX-003`, I know exactly what the address bar must do in display state and
  in edit state, and which of those two states is load-bearing for `ADR-006`.
- As a reviewer, I can tell whether a future chrome change violates the selected direction's security
  mechanism without reading the sketches.
- As the project owner, I can revisit this in six months and see what the alternatives were and what
  each would have cost.

## Acceptance criteria
1. `docs/adr/ADR-013-browser-owned-design-tokens.md` exists and records: the selected direction by
   name, every amendment to it, the amendments' rationale, and the rejected directions with why.
2. The ADR states the selected direction's `C1` mechanism concretely enough to implement, and states
   what would constitute a violation of it.
3. The ADR records the compiled-tokens rule from `C2`: `WeboraTheme` is separately compiled with no
   path from a manifest value into it, and `SiteSkinColorScheme` remains the entire
   website-influenceable colour surface.
4. `docs/adr/README.md`'s row for 013 moves from `PROPOSED` to accepted and points at the file.
5. `docs/ROADMAP.md` ticks `UX-001`.
6. No source file, test, resource or build file changes.
7. `bash scripts/pre-commit-check.sh` passes.

## NFR
- Security/privacy: the ADR is the artifact that keeps `ADR-006` enforceable through a visual
  rebuild. It must make the losing condition explicit — a direction that shows the domain inside an
  editable field, or drops the browser-authored semantics node, fails regardless of how it looks.
- Reliability/fallback: none — no runtime behaviour changes.
- Performance: none.
- Accessibility: `C5` holds unchanged. The ADR must not record any amendment that weakens the 48 dp
  target rule, the string-resource rule, or the security semantics node, since
  `BrowserSurfaceConventionsTest` enforces all three and would reject the implementation anyway.

## Risks
- **Recording a preference instead of a decision.** An ADR that names a direction without naming its
  mechanism leaves `UX-003` to reinvent the security half under delivery pressure. The `C1` section
  is the part that must be implementable as written.
- **The amendment quietly becoming a fourth direction.** Amendments must be specific and bounded, or
  the selected sketches stop describing what gets built.
- **Losing why the others lost.** Without that, the decision looks arbitrary later and gets reopened
  on taste rather than on the trade it actually made.

## Open questions
None. The direction was selected by the project owner: **A — "Soft instrument", unamended.**

The owner first selected **B — "Get out of the way"** with the fixed identity frame from
**C — "The instrument frame"**, then asked to see both directions drawn on a regular site and on the
integrated Bloom Flowers site before treating the decision as final, and reversed to A. `ADR-013`
records the reversal and its reasons; the two renders are kept in `docs/design/directions/`. Neither
selection is an open question — the earlier one is history, not a live alternative.
