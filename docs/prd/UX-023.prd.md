# UX-023: Make the integrated brand row responsive at large font scale
Status: PRD_READY

## Context / Problem

The protected integrated header's brand row holds four things: the browser's Back control, the
site's logo, the site's title, and `UX-021`'s browser-owned trust chip. At 200% font scale on a
320 dp host it cannot hold all four, and what it drops is the registrable domain — the one element
`ADR-006` requires to stay visible.

**The arithmetic, re-measured against the current row** (`SiteSkinTopBar.kt:95` `BrandRow`, which
`BROWSE-011` lifted into its own declaration without changing its contents):

| Element | Width |
|---|---|
| `BrowserBack` | 48 dp (the one `MINIMUM_TOUCH_TARGET`) |
| Spacer | 8 dp |
| `BrandLogo` | 40 dp |
| Spacer | 12 dp |
| Title column | `weight(1f)` — takes what is left |
| Spacer | 8 dp |
| `SiteSkinSecurityChip` | unweighted, `widthIn(max = 160.dp)` |

On a 320 dp host the expressive header's 20 dp gutters leave 280 dp. The fixed elements take
**116 dp**, so the title and the chip share **164 dp**. The chip is declared after the weighted
column and carries no weight, so it measures first and the title absorbs the shortfall:

- At 100% scale `example.co.uk` in the chip needs ~117 dp including shield and padding. Under the
  160 dp cap, and under 164 — everything fits.
- At 200% it needs ~198 dp. Now *available width* binds at 164 first, the chip truncates to roughly
  `example.c…`, **and the title measures to zero**.

**`SECURITY_CHIP_MAX_WIDTH` is not the binding constraint and raising it makes things worse.**
`UX-021`'s review proposed exactly that; measuring refuted it. Raising the cap to 200 dp widens the
chip by 4 dp and takes the title from 4 dp to 0 — trading the site's name for about one more
character of domain. The constant is inert at both ends of the scale. The arithmetic already lives
at its declaration (`SiteSkinTopBar.kt:314`) so the next reader hits it before reaching for the
number.

One row cannot hold four things at 200%. That is a layout problem, and this ticket is it.

**Source of record.** `docs/BACKLOG.md` § `UX-023`. There is no GitHub issue for this ticket —
it was filed from `UX-021`'s review as a backlog entry, and no issue was opened. The backlog entry
is therefore normative here, and its acceptance bullets are carried into the criteria below.

## Goals

- The integrated header stays legible at 200% font scale on a 320 dp host, with **browser identity
  and the site's title both present** rather than one starving the other.
- `ADR-006`'s guarantee holds in pixels, not only in the semantics tree: the registrable domain is
  *visible*, not truncated to a few characters and an ellipsis.
- The response to large scale is a browser decision, taken in one place, that no manifest value can
  influence or trigger.

## Non-goals

- Changing `SECURITY_CHIP_MAX_WIDTH` to fix the symptom. It is inert; see above.
- Removing the registrable domain from integrated chrome. `UX-021` considered and rejected the
  issue's bare-shield sketch on `ADR-006` grounds, and that decision stands.
- Reordering the brand row's children, or giving the chip a weight. The declaration order *is* the
  guarantee that a manifest-supplied title cannot push the trust mark out of the header.
- Redesigning the expressive header, its curve, its gutters, or `BROWSE-011`'s control row.
- Changing what the chip *says*. `UX-021`'s four `TransportSecurity` states and their one owner
  (`transportLabel`) are untouched.
- Making the regular-mode chrome responsive. Different surface, different arithmetic, its own
  ticket if it needs one.

## User stories

- As a user with 200% font scale, I can read both which site I am on (the domain Webora vouches
  for) and what the site calls itself, without either being cut to an ellipsis.
- As a user relying on `ADR-006`, the domain I am shown is the whole registrable domain, so a
  branded header cannot mislead me about which origin I am reading.
- As a site owner, my title does not vanish because a user enlarged their font.

## Acceptance criteria

1. At 200% font scale on a 320 dp host, a 13-character registrable domain (`example.co.uk`) renders
   in the trust chip **without ellipsis**.
2. At that same scale and width, the site's title is still present and non-empty.
3. The response to constrained width is a browser-owned decision expressed in one place, taking only
   browser/platform inputs; it accepts no manifest value, and a negative control proves a manifest
   cannot trigger, suppress or alter it.
4. The trust chip is still declared after the weighted title column and still carries no weight, so
   a manifest-supplied title cannot push it out of the header at any scale.
5. `SITESKIN_SECURITY_TAG` keeps its exact value and stays on the chip — `CI-009` and `CI-010`
   assert on it, including the `assertDoesNotExist` that proves `UX-012`'s teardown.
6. `UX-021`'s instrumented identity floor is **preserved or raised, never lowered**.
7. At 100% font scale the header's layout is unchanged, pinned by an assertion that fails if the
   large-scale path is taken at default scale.
8. Both `TransportSecurity` colour pairs still ground on `primaryContainer`, so no new unmeasured
   colour enters the chip; `WeboraColorSchemeTest`'s measured table is unchanged.
9. `BrowserBack` and `BROWSE-011`'s Refresh keep their 48 dp targets, their compiled icons and
   labels, and their Webora-token sub-surfaces at every scale.
10. Nothing in the renderer, event-routing, discovery, consent, capture or protocol layers is
    edited.
11. `./gradlew :app:compileDebugAndroidTestKotlin` compiles.
12. `bash scripts/pre-commit-check.sh` passes.

## NFR

- **Security/privacy:** the trust chip is the browser's claim about the origin. Any layout that can
  be influenced by manifest content — title length, subtitle presence, logo dimensions — must not be
  able to remove, shrink below legibility, or reposition it. This is `HARDEN-002`'s impersonation
  surface reached through layout instead of text, and it is the sharpest risk in the ticket.
- **Reliability/fallback:** the large-scale path must be total. A width or scale the decision did
  not anticipate falls back to a layout that still shows the domain, never to one that hides it.
- **Performance:** no measurement pass that recomposes on every frame; the decision reads inputs
  Compose already provides.
- **Accessibility:** the visible change must not regress the semantics tree — the chip keeps its
  browser-authored `contentDescription` combining transport meaning and domain, and the domain
  remains *visible*, per `ADR-006`, not merely announced. Targets stay at 48 dp.

## Risks

- **Tuning the constant instead of the layout.** The cheapest-looking fix is raising
  `SECURITY_CHIP_MAX_WIDTH`; it is measurably wrong and its arithmetic is recorded at the
  declaration. Any diff that touches that number needs to explain what it re-measured.
- **A responsive branch that reads manifest state.** "Wrap when the title is long" is the natural
  phrasing and it is a `C2` violation: it lets a website decide the browser's layout. The trigger
  must be width and scale, never content.
- **Instrumented-only evidence.** Layout at 200% scale is a device fact, and this checkout has no
  emulator. The JVM gate can hold the *decision* pure and drivable; it cannot photograph the row.
  Whatever ships must put as much as possible on the gate's side of that line.
- **`CI-009`/`CI-010` hosted frames change again.** The integrated frames already need re-taking
  after `BROWSE-011`; a header that reflows at large scale does not change the default-scale frames,
  but that must be verified rather than assumed (criterion 7).

## Open questions

- Which element yields at large scale — chip wraps to its own row, subtitle drops first, or logo
  slot shrinks? The backlog lists all three and rules only that **browser identity may never be what
  yields**. `/researcher` measures each against the 164 dp budget and the plan commits to one.
- Is the trigger available width, font scale, or both? A `BoxWithConstraints` sees width; scale is
  `LocalDensity.fontScale`. Research decides which is the honest signal.
- Does `BROWSE-011`'s control row participate? It is a sibling row with its own trailing alignment;
  a wrapped chip could join it rather than adding a third row. Research measures whether that fits.
