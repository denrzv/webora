# UX-014: Curved branded integrated top chrome
Status: PRD_READY

## Context / Problem

Integrated SiteSkin mode currently uses a conventional rectangular top app bar. UX-013 introduced
a browser-owned curved-header primitive and a closed expressive presentation projection, but left
them intentionally unwired. M9 now needs the integrated top chrome to use that foundation so the
validated site brand feels native and expressive without weakening the browser-owned security and
escape contracts established by ADR-006, UX-008, and UX-012.

## Goals

- Replace the integrated rectangular top surface with the UX-013 curved branded header primitive.
- Present the canonical domain and TLS state as a compact, always-visible browser-owned identity
  surface that remains visually and semantically separate from site branding.
- Preserve browser-observed Back, tabs/overflow, loading/error, exact-origin teardown, tab
  isolation, theme, inset, and reduced-motion behavior.
- Produce automated and visual evidence at compact width and large font scale.

## Non-goals

- Changing the persistent integrated bottom navigation or adding the SiteSkin hub; UX-015 owns it.
- Adding manifest fields, geometry, motion values, arbitrary assets, browser-command customization,
  security-label customization, or a new protocol capability.
- Changing discovery, validation, consent, action dispatch, navigation matching, or origin binding.
- Redesigning regular browser or Home chrome.

## User stories

- As a person browsing an integrated site, I want branded chrome that feels intentionally native
  while still showing which site I am trusting and whether its connection is secure.
- As a person navigating tabs or history, I want Back and browser destinations to remain reachable
  and driven only by the active tab's observed state.
- As a person using a compact device, dark theme, large text, or reduced motion, I want every
  required identity and browser control to remain visible, understandable, and operable.
- As a security reviewer, I want executable evidence that a site cannot suppress, relabel, reorder,
  restyle, or dispatch browser identity and control affordances.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Integrated mode renders its top chrome through the UX-013 curved branded header and closed
   expressive presentation projection; Home and regular modes remain unchanged.
2. The active tab's browser-observed canonical registrable domain and TLS state are always visible
   and exposed under the browser security semantics contract, separate from manifest title,
   subtitle, colours, and bounded brand asset.
3. Browser-owned Back remains first in the header, retains at least a 48 dp target, uses its existing
   browser-owned label/icon/callback, and stays visible and disabled at the history root. Tabs and
   overflow remain reachable through their existing browser-owned surfaces until UX-015 relocates
   them; none can be influenced by a manifest.
4. Loading, error, exact-origin departure, tab switching, and stale asynchronous brand-asset
   completion preserve the existing active-tab and mode-aware chrome handoff contracts; integrated
   decoration disappears before a regular or Home projection is rendered.
5. At 320 dp and 200% font scale, in light and dark themes, the header does not clip or hide the
   canonical identity or required controls, and SiteSkin colours remain contrast-guarded through
   the existing trusted projection.
6. Reduced-motion mode replaces decorative transition behavior with an immediate presentation and
   never delays identity, teardown, content, or input availability.
7. Automated negative controls fail if manifest-derived state gains a path into canonical identity,
   TLS state, browser control order/labels/icons/callbacks, header geometry, insets, or motion.
8. Available unit/instrumentation compilation gates pass; runtime instrumentation and screenshots
   are captured when a connected device exists and otherwise recorded as a managed-cloud
   environment limitation without provisioning a software-only emulator.
9. `bash scripts/pre-commit-check.sh` passes.

## NFR
- Security/privacy: no new network, persistence, telemetry, manifest, or native-capability surface;
  canonical identity and browser actions remain browser observed and browser owned.
- Reliability/fallback: existing regular-mode fallback and exact-origin teardown remain total;
  decorative brand-asset failure falls back immediately to the browser-produced monogram.
- Performance: no WebView or navigation blocking; presentation derives synchronously from already
  trusted active-tab state and bounded local/accepted asset state.
- Accessibility: stable semantics, non-colour TLS labeling, 48 dp targets, deterministic reading
  order, 320 dp/200% font support, and immediate reduced-motion behavior.

## Risks

- A larger expressive surface could crowd identity at compact width; adaptive browser-owned layout
  must preserve identity and commands rather than conditionally omit them.
- Site colours around identity could make browser ownership ambiguous; identity and controls need a
  browser-token sub-surface or equivalent explicit separation inside the branded container.
- Recomposition or delayed brand assets could leak inactive-tab styling; composition must project
  only the active tab and retain configuration-identity association.
- Replacing the top bar could accidentally fork mode or navigation logic; integration must consume
  the existing exhaustive chrome handoff and controller callbacks rather than introduce booleans.

## Open questions

- Research must determine the smallest adaptive layout that preserves brand, identity, Back, tabs,
  and overflow at 320 dp/200% without widening manifest influence.
- Research must decide whether existing loading/error presentation needs relocation or only proof
  that its current contract survives the header replacement.
