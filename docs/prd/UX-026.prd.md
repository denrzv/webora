# UX-026: Make the SiteSkin drawer easy to dismiss and size it to content
Status: PRD_READY

Issue: [#121](https://github.com/denrzv/webora/issues/121)

## Context / Problem

`UX-022` chose a `Dialog(usePlatformDefaultWidth = false)` for the hub drawer, and chose it for a
structural reason that still holds: a dialog composes into its own window, so Back is consumed there
before `BrowserBackHandler` ever sees it, and `BROWSE-002`'s single Back contract needs no second
handler racing it. That decision is not reopened here.

What the same shape did **not** buy is the other half of modal dismissal. Two defects follow, and
they are one root cause apart:

1. **Outside-tap dismissal is inert.** `DialogProperties.dismissOnClickOutside` defaults to true and
   `DialogWrapper.onTouchEvent` honours it — but only for a touch that
   `DialogLayout.isInsideContent` says falls outside the composed content's bounds. The drawer's
   content is a `Box(Modifier.fillMaxSize())`, so the content *is* the window and no touch can ever
   be outside it. `SITESKIN_HUB_SCRIM_TAG` names a rectangle that looks like a scrim, absorbs the
   tap, and does nothing with it. The only reliable way to close the drawer today is to select a
   site item, so a user who opened the menu to look at it must navigate to get out.
2. **The panel is `fillMaxHeight()`.** Bloom, after `UX-025` moved Catalog, Cart and Profile into the
   dock, has two rows left in the drawer. They are drawn at the vertical centre of a full-height
   panel with the rest of the viewport left blank, which reads as a surface that failed to load
   rather than a compact site menu.

The second defect is cosmetic; the first is the ticket. A modal whose documented dismissal gesture
is wired to nothing is worse than one with no scrim at all, because the affordance is present and
the user's correct action produces no result.

## What the hosted journey does and does not prove

`CI-010` is `QA_PENDING_HOSTED_ACCEPTANCE`, and the history matters because it is the evidence a
reviewer would otherwise reach for:

| Run | Commit | Frame 05 reached the dock by | Proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 44 | `90b5d7f` (`UX-024`) | clicking a **drawer row** | the drawer closes when you navigate — the complaint, not the fix |
| 45 | `4d8a3f0` (`UX-025`) | `SITESKIN_HUB_SCRIM_TAG.performClick()` | first run to exercise the scrim path at all; in progress when this PRD was written |

So no completed hosted run has ever demonstrated scrim dismissal, and `UX-025` introduced the step
that assumes it. Whatever run 45 reports, the mechanism has to be built rather than assumed.

## Goals

- An open drawer is dismissible by tapping outside it, by a browser-owned mechanism that does not
  depend on a framework hit-test the drawer's own layout defeats.
- Dismissal without dispatch: closing the drawer must not select an item, navigate, or reach
  `ActionResolver`.
- Back keeps closing the drawer before browser/WebView history is evaluated, by the same structural
  route `UX-022` chose.
- Drawer height follows its content between a browser-owned minimum and maximum, scrolling at the
  maximum rather than clipping or truncating.
- Every `UX-022`/`UX-025` guarantee — trusted-item dispatch, de-duplication, group omission, the
  single overlay state and its three resets, the absent trust surface — survives unchanged.

## Non-goals

- Reopening `UX-022`'s `Dialog`-over-`ModalNavigationDrawer` decision.
- Any manifest-reachable dimension, offset, animation, dismissal rule or scrim.
- Changing the `BOUQUET` presentation, the hint vocabulary, or `resolveHubPresentation`.
- Changing the dock's three slots, their order, or `UX-025`'s projection.
- A swipe-to-dismiss gesture. The issue permits it "where supported by the chosen Compose
  interaction model"; the chosen model is a dialog window with no drag state, and adding one would
  need a drag/velocity model competing with the panel's own scroll. Scrim and Back are two
  independent dismissal paths and the issue requires neither to be the only one.
- Focus restoration to a specific browser control. The dialog window returns focus to the browser
  window on dismissal by construction; a chosen restoration target is a separate accessibility
  ticket if a device ever shows it is needed.

## User stories

- As a user, I open the site menu, decide against it, tap outside, and I am on the page I was on.
- As a user with a screen reader, Back closes the menu and does not take me back a page.
- As a Bloom user, the menu is the size of the menu.
- As a site owner with twenty menu entries, every one of them is still reachable.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Tapping the scrim closes the drawer through a browser-owned mechanism whose behaviour does not
   depend on `DialogLayout.isInsideContent`, and a negative control proves the framework path alone
   does not close it.
2. A scrim tap dispatches nothing: no `onSelect`, no `NavigationItem`, no navigation.
3. A tap on the panel — on a row, on a heading, or on empty panel space — does not close the drawer
   through the scrim.
4. Android and predictive Back still close the drawer before `BrowserBackHandler` runs, and the
   drawer still installs no `BackHandler` of its own.
5. A subsequent Back, with the drawer closed, follows the ordinary browser Back policy unchanged.
6. Drawer height is decided by one pure, browser-owned function of platform facts, drivable by the
   JVM gate, taking no manifest value of any kind.
7. Content shorter than the maximum produces a panel that wraps its content; a Bloom-shaped drawer
   occupies materially less than the full viewport.
8. Content taller than the maximum produces a panel bounded at the maximum whose content scrolls to
   its last row; nothing is dropped, capped or truncated to stay compact.
9. Below the minimum, the panel is at least the browser-owned minimum height.
10. The panel stays within the safe-drawing area; system bars and gesture insets are respected once,
    with no second inset read.
11. Empty groups still render no heading and reserve no space, per `UX-022`.
12. Selecting a row still closes the drawer *before* dispatching the original trusted
    `NavigationItem` through the existing path.
13. Tab switch, exact-origin departure and configuration replacement still close the drawer through
    the one `IntegratedOverlay` state and its existing resets; no second visibility state is added.
14. No manifest value — item count, label length, colours, icons, the `presentation` object or its
    `hub`/`dock` fields — can influence panel height, minimum, maximum, alignment, scrim behaviour or
    dismissal; a negative control proves it.
15. `BOUQUET` presentation, the dock's three slots and their order, and `UX-025`'s projection and
    de-duplication are unchanged.
16. 320 dp width, 200% font scale, RTL start-side placement and both colour projections remain
    operable, with 48 dp targets intact.
17. The hosted journey's frame 04 keeps its `UX-022` acceptance checks and its scrim dismissal step
    now rests on a mechanism that exists.
18. `./gradlew :app:compileDebugAndroidTestKotlin` compiles.
19. `bash scripts/pre-commit-check.sh` passes.

## NFR

- **Security/privacy:** the drawer's geometry and dismissal policy are browser-owned. The sharpest
  risk in this ticket is a height rule that reads the number of site items — that would let a
  manifest decide how much of the browser's window it occupies, which is a site-controlled dimension
  arriving through a count instead of a dp. The rule must take platform facts only, exactly as
  `headerIdentityPlacement` takes width, scale and a browser-derived domain length.
- **Reliability/fallback:** every failure direction ends with the drawer closable. A height
  computation that receives a degenerate available height must still produce a usable panel, and no
  change here may make an item unreachable.
- **Performance:** pure layout arithmetic; no new network, storage, asset or measurement pass.
- **Accessibility:** two independent dismissal paths, neither of them the scrim alone; 48 dp targets;
  scrolling rather than clipping at 200%; RTL start-side by layout direction.

## Risks

- **A scrim that swallows a tap it does not act on.** The current defect in one sentence. A
  `clickable` scrim fixes it and introduces the mirror risk: a tap on the panel falling through to
  the scrim and closing the drawer under the user's finger. Both directions need a test.
- **The scrim becoming an accessibility node.** A full-screen clickable rectangle with a role and a
  label is a large target a screen reader will announce and traverse. It must be dismiss-only and
  must not present itself as a control competing with the panel's rows.
- **Height derived from content count.** The obvious implementation reads `model.siteMenu.size`. That
  is criterion 14's violation and would pass every visual check.
- **The hosted journey.** `CI-010` is already pending acceptance and this ticket changes the frame it
  photographs. Acceptance must be re-taken and cannot be replaced by compiled instrumentation.
- **`assertIsDisplayed()` over a clipped panel.** `UX-009` and `UX-023` both record that semantics
  keep full text and unclipped bounds, so a sizing regression can pass a presence assertion. The
  sizing evidence has to be bounds, not presence.

## Open questions

- Does the panel stay vertically centred, or move to the top edge? The issue's sketch shows content
  at the top with "remaining viewport belongs to modal scrim" below. Research decides, and the answer
  changes where a scrim tap can land.
- Can `windowInsetsPadding(WindowInsets.safeDrawing)` stay on the content column, or must it move
  outward once the panel is content-sized? Research must check where the inset is currently consumed
  and whether the panel can be pushed under a system bar.
- Is a max-height fraction of the available height enough, or does the maximum need the same
  `fontScale` input `headerIdentityPlacement` takes? Research should say whether large text changes
  what "maximum" should mean.
- Did hosted run 45 pass frame 05? Its answer is evidence about the current mechanism, not a
  dependency: the ticket proceeds either way.
