# UX-004: Home, onboarding and settings surfaces
Status: PRD_READY

## Context / Problem
The browser-owned home, onboarding, and privacy settings surfaces still use the pre-refresh layout even though `UX-002` established Webora's compiled design tokens and `UX-003` rebuilt the browsing chrome. The roadmap makes these three remaining surfaces the final M6 design-refresh ticket.

The work is visual and structural, not behavioural. Home must retain safe address resolution and browser-owned suggestions; onboarding must retain its saved three-step flow; privacy settings must preserve the global SiteSkin control, canonical per-origin decisions, clear-data confirmation, and close actions. Website or manifest data cannot influence Webora tokens.

## Goals
1. Apply Direction A and `WeboraTheme` tokens consistently to home, onboarding, and privacy settings.
2. Give home a useful, scannable hierarchy for search, recents, favourites, and suggested integrations.
3. Present onboarding as a resilient browser-authored progression with clear step and action hierarchy.
4. Turn privacy controls into grouped settings rows, including compact per-origin reset actions.
5. Preserve accessibility contracts at large font scales and document changed surfaces.

## Non-goals
- New browsing history, favourites persistence, settings, privacy behaviours, or SiteSkin protocol fields.
- Changes to browsing chrome, SiteSkin integrated chrome, consent, manifest validation, or networking.
- Remote icons, remote colours, analytics, or a new UI dependency.

## User stories
- As a returning user, I can recognise Webora and quickly enter a destination or open a safe suggested integration.
- As a first-time user, I can understand the product's browser-owned trust boundary and complete or skip onboarding.
- As a privacy-conscious user, I can understand the global SiteSkin state, review complete canonical origins, reset one decision, clear browsing data, or close settings.
- As a screen-reader or large-text user, I can identify every action and state without relying on colour, clipping, or visual adjacency.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Home, onboarding, and privacy settings use `WeboraTheme` colour, typography, spacing, shape, and local icon/button primitives without direct Material defaults or manifest-derived styling.
2. Home retains safe address submission and all browser-owned suggested destinations, while recents/favourites empty states and suggestions have a clear Direction A hierarchy.
3. Onboarding retains all three pages, step position, Skip, Next, and Start behaviour in a scrollable layout that remains usable at 200% font scale.
4. The global SiteSkin setting is a single labelled list row whose switch retains an explicit `stateDescription` and accessible name.
5. Each stored decision shows its complete canonical origin visibly and in the reset action's accessible name; reset is no longer a full-width filled button labelled only by the origin.
6. Clear-browsing-data, close, and confirmation behaviours remain unchanged and browser-owned.
7. Every interactive target is at least 48 dp, resource-backed copy is used, and relevant instrumentation tests compile; runtime tests run when a device is available.
8. `docs/accessibility/CONFORMANCE.md` describes the accessibility contracts of all three changed surfaces.
9. `bash scripts/pre-commit-check.sh` passes.

## NFR
- Security/privacy: browser tokens and destinations remain compiled; complete canonical origins remain legible; no new telemetry or URL logging.
- Reliability: this restyle cannot change stores, navigation resolution, data clearing, consent decisions, or fallback behaviour.
- Accessibility: 48 dp targets, named controls, explicit toggle state, complete-origin identification, scrolling, and 200% font-scale resilience.
- Performance: no new dependency, remote asset, or work triggered by recomposition.

## Risks
- A compact reset icon can lose the origin context; keep the full origin visible and include it in the action's accessible name.
- A clickable settings row plus nested switch can create duplicate or conflicting actions; expose one coherent toggle target.
- Decorative hierarchy can clip at 200% font scale; use scrolling and wrapping rather than fixed-height text containers.
- Restyling empty sections can imply persistence that does not exist; continue to label them as empty.

## Open questions
None. Scope and visual direction are fixed by the M6 roadmap, backlog, `UX-002`, and `ADR-013`.
