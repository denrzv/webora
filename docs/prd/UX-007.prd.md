# UX-007: Adaptive SiteSkin consent action hierarchy
Status: PRD_READY

## Context / Problem

The first-use SiteSkin dialog presents the correct protected origin, bounded attributed preview,
and three browser-owned decisions, but delegates those decisions to `AlertDialog`'s independent
confirm and dismiss slots. `Allow` occupies the confirm slot while `Not now` and `Never for this
site` share an unspaced `FlowRow` in the dismiss slot. On a phone width this can split into an
accidental-looking isolated action and row; long labels and 200% font scale make the hierarchy less
legible precisely where the user is making a persistent trust decision.

## Goals

1. Make Allow unmistakably primary, Not now secondary, and Never for this site a lower-emphasis
   persistent-denial action.
2. Use one deliberate adaptive layout that stacks/reflows all three actions together.
3. Keep every action readable, reachable, and at least 48 dp at narrow widths and 200% font scale.
4. Preserve the exact origin, protected explanation, attributed preview, and consent persistence.

## Non-goals

- Changing consent choices, callbacks, storage, activation, origin binding, or dismissal semantics.
- Allowing a manifest to influence labels, order, emphasis, colour, sizing, or layout.
- Changing the SiteSkin preview or integrated chrome.

## Acceptance criteria

1. All three actions are rendered by one browser-owned adaptive action component in fixed order and
   visual hierarchy: Allow primary, Not now secondary, Never for this site persistent denial.
2. The actions form an intentional full-width stack on compact phone widths and remain readable,
   reachable, and at least 48 dp at 200% font scale and with long test labels.
3. The protected canonical origin and browser/security message remain unchanged and cannot be
   rewritten or styled by manifest data.
4. Allow, Not now, Never, and outside dismissal preserve their existing callbacks and persistence
   semantics.
5. Compose coverage pins hierarchy, ordering, narrow-width reflow, long labels, 200% font scale,
   minimum targets, and all callbacks.
6. Pixel 6 / API 33 consent evidence shows no clipping or awkward split when runtime capture is
   available.
7. `bash scripts/pre-commit-check.sh` passes.

## NFR

- **Security:** action content and presentation are compiled browser policy; manifest data remains
  confined to `SiteSkinConsentModel` and cannot enter the action component.
- **Accessibility:** controls retain semantic button roles, minimum targets, readable labels, and
  deterministic focus/visual order without shrinking text.
- **Reliability:** adaptive layout does not depend on measuring manifest content or device-specific
  branching and the dialog remains scrollable.

## Risks

- Material `AlertDialog` may impose separate confirm/dismiss arrangement. Mitigate by providing the
  complete action hierarchy through one slot and leaving the other empty.
- A generic reusable component could invite non-consent policy into its API. Keep it private and
  accept only callbacks and browser-authored label resources.
