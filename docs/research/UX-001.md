# UX-001: Research
Status: RESEARCH_READY

## Question
What does selecting direction **B** commit `M6` to, what does the fixed-identity-frame amendment
change about it, and which parts of the selection are load-bearing for `ADR-006` rather than
cosmetic?

## Origins involved
None. This ticket touches no origin, no manifest and no network path. Recorded because the ticket
sits inside a project where most decisions are origin-shaped and this one is not.

## Manifest-controlled surface
**None, and that is the point of `C2`.** `SiteSkinColorScheme` (`SiteSkinTheme.kt:42`) is the entire
website-influenceable colour surface. Everything `M6` builds sits outside it. The invariant is
currently maintained only by there being no browser palette at all — once `UX-002` creates one, the
invariant needs a test, which is why the ADR must state it now rather than leave it implied.

## Browser-owned remainder
Everything in `M6`, and specifically the part `C1` protects:

`BrowserSecurityIdentity` reads `securityPresentation(state.mode)`, derived from the **committed
`SiteOrigin`** — never `state.addressText`, never document content, never a manifest field. The
dedicated semantics node carrying `BROWSER_SECURITY_TAG` is half the guarantee; the visible row is
the other half. Assistive technology reads the tree, not the pixels.

## The selection

**B — "Get out of the way"**, amended with **C — "The instrument frame"**'s fixed identity colour.

| Property | Direction B as sketched |
|---|---|
| Chrome | 48 dp omnibox + 52 dp toolbar, hairline-only elevation |
| Palette | Near-monochrome; action `#1552C4` light / `#8AB4F8` dark, TLS secure `#146C43` |
| Geometry | Radius 8 dp controls, 10 dp cards; 4 dp base spacing, 16 dp gutter |
| Type | 22 / 16 / 14 / 13 / 12, Roboto |
| Icons | 8, 20 dp, stroke 1.9 |
| Strength | Most page area of the three; zero learning cost; densest at 200% font scale |
| Cost | "Webora looks like every other browser — no identity of its own" |
| Effort | **Highest** of the three, because of the two-state omnibox |

### Why B is the highest-effort direction, and it is not the styling

B's `C1` mechanism is two states, and the second is mandatory:

1. **Display state** — the omnibox is *not a text field*. It renders `securityPresentation(state.mode)`
   as non-editable browser-owned text, path in a dimmer weight.
2. **Edit state** — tapping swaps in the real editable field, and the identity moves to a separate
   row beneath it, so an editable value and an identity claim are never the same pixels.

The sketch is unambiguous: *"If that second state is dropped for simplicity, this direction violates
`ADR-006`. It is the single most important thing to hold onto if B is chosen."* This is the entire
reason B is riskier than A or C, and it is an implementation cost, not a visual one — which is
exactly the kind of thing that gets dropped late under delivery pressure.

### What the amendment changes

C's central idea is that *the browser's frame is always the browser's*: the identity bar is the same
graphite in light and dark and never takes a colour from anything, so the boundary between what
Webora says and what the site says is visible rather than documented.

Applied to B, this addresses B's stated cost — no identity of its own — without adding chrome
height, which is the one thing B is optimising away. It costs one extra token concept: a colour
outside the light/dark pair. C's own audit notes this makes contrast checking *simpler*, since the
frame is checked once against a fixed colour rather than per theme.

The amendment also reinforces `C1` rather than merely decorating it: an identity surface that can
never take a site's colour is harder to confuse with site chrome in SiteSkin mode.

## Directions not selected

## Prior art
- `ADR-006` — the domain and TLS indicator are browser-owned and non-suppressible. `C1` is its
  application to a visual rebuild.
- `HARDEN-002` — brand-impersonation controls; the semantics node is from there.
- `A11Y-001` — the conventions gate that will enforce whatever `UX-003` writes.
- `SKIN-001` — the six-role projection and contrast guard that the browser conspicuously lacks, and
  the reason `M6` was opened at all.

## Risks
- **The two-state omnibox is dropped late.** → The ADR must state it as the condition of the
  selection, not as a design note, and name the violation explicitly.
- **"Fixed identity frame" is under-specified and drifts.** → The ADR must say it is one colour
  outside the light/dark pair, applied to the identity surface, never sourced from a manifest.
- **`C2` is asserted and never tested.** → The ADR records the rule; `UX-002` carries the test. Noted
  as a handoff rather than left implicit.

## Open questions
None. Direction and amendment are decided; the remaining choices belong to `UX-002` and `UX-003`.
