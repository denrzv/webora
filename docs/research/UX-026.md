# UX-026 research — dismissing and sizing the SiteSkin hub drawer
Status: RESEARCH_READY

PRD: `docs/prd/UX-026.prd.md`
Issue: [#121](https://github.com/denrzv/webora/issues/121)
Measured at `4d8a3f0` (`UX-025`), Compose UI **1.10.6** as resolved by the current Gradle cache.

## Question

Two things the plan must decide before it can name a file: **by what mechanism a tap outside the
panel closes it** — the framework's own is provably inert here — and **what decides the panel's
height**, given that the obvious input is a manifest-controlled count.

## F1 — `dismissOnClickOutside` cannot fire for this drawer, and that is provable rather than
suspected

`SiteSkinHubDrawer.kt:80` passes `DialogProperties(usePlatformDefaultWidth = false)`. Every other
field takes its default, so `dismissOnClickOutside` is `true`. The dialog's touch handling was read
out of the resolved artifact rather than from memory:

```
$ javap -p -c androidx/compose/ui/window/DialogWrapper.class    # ui-android 1.10.6
public boolean onTouchEvent(android.view.MotionEvent);
   ...
   10: invokevirtual DialogProperties.getDismissOnClickOutside:()Z
   13: ifeq  105                       # feature off -> skip
   21: invokevirtual DialogLayout.isInsideContent:(Landroid/view/MotionEvent;)Z
   24: ifne  105                       # inside content -> skip
   ...  ACTION_UP with isPressOutside -> onDismissRequest.invoke()
```

and `DialogLayout.isInsideContent` resolves the rectangle from **`getChildAt(0)`** — the composed
content's own bounds, not the window's:

```
   56: getChildAt:(I)Landroid/view/View;      # first child
   69: getLeft() + child.getLeft() ... + child.getWidth() / getHeight()
```

The drawer's content is `Box(Modifier.fillMaxSize())` (`SiteSkinHubDrawer.kt:88`). With
`usePlatformDefaultWidth = false` that box *is* the window, so `isInsideContent` is true for every
touch the window receives and the dismissal branch is unreachable. **`SITESKIN_HUB_SCRIM_TAG` names
a rectangle that looks like a scrim, absorbs the tap and does nothing with it.**

The consequence the issue reports follows directly: the only reliable close is selecting a row,
which dispatches.

## F3 — `performClick()` clicks the node's centre, which makes the journey's step content-dependent

`SITESKIN_HUB_SCRIM_TAG` is on a full-screen `Box`, so its centre is the screen's centre. Once the
panel is content-sized and top-aligned, the centre is scrim for a short menu and **panel** for a menu
taller than half the viewport. A step that works for Bloom and silently stops testing dismissal for a
larger site is the kind of assertion `CI-005` refuses. The plan should pin the tap to a point that is
outside the panel for every permitted content height, not to the node's default centre.

## F4 — The panel is unconditionally full height, and Bloom fills a third of it

`SiteSkinHubDrawer.kt:92` is `Modifier.fillMaxHeight()`. What the reference integration puts in it,
after `UX-025` moved three ids into the dock (`spec/fixtures/valid/bloom-flowers.json`):

| Group | Items after de-duplication |
|---|---|
| Quick actions | `call-shop` |
| Site menu (`bottomNavigation`) | `home` |
| More from site (`menu`) | — (empty, omitted) |

Estimated content height at 100% scale, from the component's own tokens (`GUTTER` 20 ×2,
`HUB_BRAND_SLOT_SIZE` 48, `LARGE` 16 between groups, heading ~20 + `SMALL` 8 + `MINIMUM_TOUCH_TARGET`
48 per group):

```
40 + 48 + 16 + (20 + 8 + 48) + 16 + (20 + 8 + 48)  ≈  272 dp
```

The hosted device is a Pixel 6 profile: 1080 × 2400 at 2.75 ≈ **393 × 873 dp**, roughly **800 dp** of
safe height. So the panel draws 272 dp of content in an 800 dp box — **two thirds of it blank**.

## F5 — A maximum below 100% is a dismissal requirement, not a taste

If the panel may grow to the full viewport, a site with twenty `menu` entries leaves no scrim to tap
and Back becomes the only path out. A browser-owned maximum **fraction** keeps a strip of scrim
reachable at every content height, which is what makes criterion 1 hold for a long menu rather than
only for Bloom. This is the reason the maximum is a fraction of available height rather than a fixed
dp: a constant is either too tall on a small device or wasteful on a large one, and neither keeps the
guarantee.

## F6 — Font scale changes the content, not the browser's willingness to yield the window

The natural reflex after `UX-023` is to feed `fontScale` into the height rule. It does not belong:
large text makes the *content* taller, and the response to taller content is already scrolling at the
maximum. How much of its own window the browser is willing to give a site menu is a policy that does
not change because the user enlarged the text. Taking `fontScale` would add an input that changes
nothing and invite a future reader to make it change something.

## F7 — The inset is already consumed, twice, and one of them is probably a no-op

`DialogProperties.decorFitsSystemWindows` defaults to `true` (confirmed in the same artifact), so the
dialog's window content area already excludes the system bars; the
`windowInsetsPadding(WindowInsets.safeDrawing)` at `SiteSkinHubDrawer.kt:100` is defensive and
currently near-zero. `BrowserScreen` consumes `safeDrawing` once for the browser's own window
(`UX-013`), and a dialog is a different window, so these do not compound.

Once the panel is content-sized the padding's placement stops being cosmetic: applied *inside* the
panel it inflates the panel's height by the inset; applied to the box that measures the available
height it reduces the space the panel is sized against, which is what the maximum should mean. The
plan should move it outward and keep exactly one read.

## F8 — Compose pointer dispatch is what makes a scrim safe or unsafe, and the direction matters

A `clickable` scrim is the obvious fix and brings two problems. It publishes a full-screen node with
a click role that assistive technology will announce and traverse — a control competing with the
panel's own rows — and, being an **ancestor** of the panel, it is on the hit path for taps on the
panel too, so an empty strip of panel would dismiss under the user's finger.

Compose's Main pass runs **children before parents**, and `detectTapGestures` awaits an unconsumed
down. So the safe shape is:

- scrim: `pointerInput { detectTapGestures { onDismiss() } }` — a gesture, contributing no semantics
  node and no accessible role;
- panel: a pointer-input node that consumes what its children did not, so the ancestor's detector
  never sees a tap that landed on the panel.

Both halves are needed, and the second is the one a reviewer would leave out.

## F9 — What the JVM gate can and cannot reach here

The gate compiles neither `androidTest` nor composition. Everything decided in a `@Composable` is
therefore invisible to it — `BROWSE-009`, `BROWSE-010`, `UX-023` and `UX-024` all record the same
answer: **a pure function plus a thin composable that reads the platform value.** `headerIdentityPlacement`
is the closest precedent in the same package, and it takes platform facts plus a browser-derived
length precisely so a site cannot reach it.

So the height rule is a pure function of available height, and the mechanisms that cannot leave a
composable (which alignment, which modifier, what the panel consumes) are covered by a source
contract test on executable lines only, in the file `SiteSkinHubDrawerContractTest` already owns.

## Origins involved

None new. The drawer renders the already-trusted configuration of the **one** integrated origin the
browser committed to; the exact-origin activation and teardown guards in `SKIN-004`/`UX-012` are
untouched, and no network, asset or storage path is added. The brand asset continues to arrive as an
already-published `BrandAsset` parameter — `NET-003`'s same-origin recheck and `NET-004`'s
publication guard are not re-entered.

## Manifest-controlled surface

Unchanged, and this ticket must keep it that way. A site supplies:

- item ids, labels, icons and typed actions — already bounded by `SiteSkinChromeModel` and
  `accessibleLabel`;
- colours, already through `SKIN-001`'s contrast guard;
- `presentation.hub`, which selects among compiled components (`UX-022`);
- `presentation.dock`, which nominates already-validated ids (`UX-025`).

Nothing in that list may reach a dimension. The sharp risk is height derived from item count: it is
site-controlled, it reads as a layout detail, and it would pass every visual check.

## Browser-owned remainder

Panel height, minimum and maximum; the maximum fraction and therefore how much scrim survives;
alignment; the scrim and its gesture; what Back does; whether the panel scrolls; the 48 dp targets;
and the absence of any identity surface inside the panel (`UX-021`, `ADR-006`).

## Risks

- **The scrim swallowing a tap it does not act on** → the plan names the gesture explicitly and the
  contract test asserts it is present, with a negative control that removing it leaves the framework
  path unable to close the drawer (F1 is why that control bites).
- **A tap on empty panel space dismissing** → the panel must consume; both halves of F8 are
  obligations, not one.
- **Height from item count** → the rule takes `Dp` and nothing else, and a reflective assertion over
  its parameter types keeps it that way as it grows.
- **The scrim announced as a control** → a gesture, never `clickable`; asserted structurally.
- **A long menu with no reachable scrim** → the maximum is a fraction below 1 (F5), asserted.
- **The journey's centre tap becoming content-dependent** → F3; pin the point.
- **Hosted acceptance** → `CI-010` is already pending and this changes its frame. It must be
  re-taken, and compiled instrumentation cannot stand in for it.
